what is cosmetic gynaecology?

A specialised subspecialty of aesthetic surgical correction

Cosmetic Gynaecology is a subspecialty of aesthetic surgical corrections that addresses women's anatomical and functional changes to the pelvic floor. This area of elective surgery for women involves specialists in gynaecology, urology, urogynaecology, and plastic surgery.

Changes to the pelvic floor over time — through ageing, pregnancy, and childbirth — may affect the internal and external structure and appearance of the vagina. Cosmetic gynaecology enables women to improve the aesthetic appearance and function of the vulvo-vaginal region and labia.

This subspecialty offers a range of procedures to address different concerns experienced by women.

Specialised procedures we offer

A range of surgical and non-surgical procedures performed by experienced gynaecology, urology and aesthetic specialists.

Labiaplasty addresses the size (enlargement) and shape (asymmetry) of the labia majora and labia minora, which can cause discomfort during sexual intercourse, exercise, and sports, as well as recurring urinary tract infections and self-consciousness. It is one of the most well-known procedures in this field. This outpatient procedure can be performed to improve function or for aesthetic purposes to support the patient's psychological wellbeing.

Vaginal muscles are elastic — they stretch (during childbirth) and gradually contract afterwards. However, due to hormonal changes, ageing, and events like childbirth, contraction may not occur fully, and women may experience Vaginal Laxity Syndrome (VLS). To treat VLS, a vaginal tightening procedure is performed.

Vaginal tightening can be done two ways:

  • Surgical (Vaginoplasty) — involves surgical intervention to remove tissue inside the vagina. This is reserved for extreme cases where muscle tearing has occurred.
  • Non-surgical — uses laser and radiofrequency to trigger collagen formation, naturally resulting in tightening.

Urinary incontinence is the involuntary leakage of urine — a highly prevalent and embarrassing condition that affects quality of life, socialising, and physio-psychological wellbeing. Contributing factors include menopause, age, childbirth, and genetics.

Treatment is mostly non-surgical, with surgical intervention reserved for extreme cases. Modern non-invasive options include:

  • BTL Emsella Chair — an electromagnetic device that strengthens weak pelvic floor muscles by producing supramaximal Kegel exercises.
  • FemiLift Laser — induces collagen renewal, leading to tissue remodelling and rejuvenation.

Vaginismus involves involuntary tightening of the vaginal muscles, often causing pain and difficulty with penetration. Treatment approaches include muscle-relaxing therapies, pelvic floor physiotherapy, and targeted neurotoxin (Botox) injections to relax the affected muscles, alongside counselling support where appropriate.
